MakerVerse 2027 | Four schools, one city

Build with AI. Solve for Real.

"AI can help you build almost anything. The question is whether what you build actually helps someone."

Students from Cumbres, Columbus, Montessori and Marymount will team up to tackle real environmental problems in Medellín, linked to the UN Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs). This year, the challenges are given to you: each one is a real problem with a clear brief, and you choose the problem inside it that interests you most.

You will use AI-assisted coding ("vibe coding") and other AI tools to go from idea to working prototype fast. Because AI makes building easy, we judge two things above all: real impact (does it work, and can you prove it?) and what you learn (can you explain what you built, and have you learned to use AI tools well and critically?).

What is Design Thinking?

A solution-based approach to solving problems. Useful for tackling complex problems that are ill-defined or unknown. In MakerVerse it also protects you from the biggest risk of vibe coding: building something impressive that nobody needs.

1. Empathize 2. Define 3. Ideate 4. Prototype 5. Test

1. Empathize

Observe, measure and talk to the people affected. Do not guess: find real data about your problem.

2. Define

Write your problem as a "How might we..." question that you can actually test.

3. Ideate

Brainstorm many ideas, then choose the one that can have the most real impact in the time you have.

4. Prototype

Build it with AI-assisted coding and your hardware kit. Keep a log of how you used AI.

5. Test & Present

Prove it works with measurements or tests with real users, then explain the problem, the process, the solution and the impact.

Thinking Styles

  • Divergent Thinking: Creating many different ideas.
  • Convergent Thinking: Choosing the best few ideas.

Curiosity Tips

  • Seek inspiration beyond related contexts.
  • Adopt a beginner's mindset.
  • Ask "Why" and "What if".
  • Frame problems as "How might we...".

The Challenges

Every challenge is a real environmental problem in our city. Inside each challenge you will find several problems that can be solved on their own. Your team picks one and goes deep.

The goal is impact, not inventions. A device is only a tool for changing something real. Before you build anything, answer these four questions, and keep coming back to them:

  • Who is affected, and who would be better off?
  • What has to change (a decision, a habit, a place) for their life to improve?
  • Who has the power to make that change happen, and how will you convince them?
  • How will we know it worked? Which number or observation tells us?

Whatever you choose, you must show evidence that it works: measurements, data or tests with real users.

Challenge 1 | Engineering

Clean Air in the Aburrá Valley

SDG 3 Good Health & Well-being | SDG 11 Sustainable Cities & Communities | SDG 13 Climate Action

Medellín lives with air pollution, and most of us do not know what the air at our own school looks like, or what to do about it. You will not be judged on how clever your device is, but on whether something real changes because of your work. Pick one of the three problems below.

1A. Making the Invisible Air Visible

How might we help our school community make better decisions on days when the air is bad?

  • The problem: official stations are far away, and nobody at school knows whether today is a good day to do PE outdoors or to keep the windows open. Data that nobody sees or trusts changes nothing.
  • Impact goal: students, teachers and coordinators change a real decision (for example moving PE indoors or ventilating differently) because they received a clear, trusted alert.
  • Your mission: find out who needs to know what, when, and in what form. Then build the lowest-cost way to measure and alert, with levels you define and justify. Agree with a real decision-maker (a teacher or coordinator) what the school does at each alert.
  • Deliver: a working station, an action protocol agreed with a decision-maker, the total cost in pesos, and a pilot of at least one to two weeks.
  • Prove the impact: data compared with an official source (so people can trust it), and a record of the decisions that changed during the pilot.

1B. Cleaner Air in Every Classroom

How might we give every classroom cleaner air at a cost a school can actually afford?

  • The problem: with windows open, polluted street air comes in (including dust from construction work near the school); with windows closed, CO2 and particles build up. Commercial purifiers are too expensive to put in every room, so most students breathe whatever the room gives them.
  • Impact goal: many classrooms, not just one prototype, end up with measurably cleaner air, because your solution is cheap and simple enough for others to copy.
  • Your mission: design a low-cost way to clean classroom air, but think beyond the first unit: who would build the rest, how much would it cost, and could another school do it too? Test it in a real room and improve it.
  • Deliver: a tested prototype, a build guide that someone outside your team could follow, the cost per classroom, and a plan to equip the rest of the school.
  • Prove the impact: particle measurements before and after in a test classroom, the improvement you get per peso spent, and notes on noise, power use and electrical safety.

Safety: any test with a fan or mains electricity must be supervised by a mentor first.

1C. Breathe Easier at the Gate

How might we reduce the pollution students breathe while they wait to arrive or be picked up?

  • The problem: at arrival and pick-up, buses, cars and motorbikes gather with engines running, right where students wait. Drivers and families rarely know they are part of the problem.
  • Impact goal: a measurable drop in what students breathe at the gate, because the people who cause the pollution change what they do.
  • Your mission: understand who is at the gate and why engines stay on. Measure when and where the air is worst, then design and test an intervention that changes behavior (a waiting zone, a schedule, signs, a message to families). Measuring is how you find the problem and prove the change; the change itself is your goal.
  • Deliver: a map or graph of the pollution peaks, a tested intervention, and the message or plan you would take to the school administration.
  • Prove the impact: measurements before and after your intervention, plus a count of behavior change (for example how many drivers turned engines off).

Team Roles and Responsibilities

Project Manager

  • Oversees the whole process.
  • Distributes tasks and meets deadlines.
  • Liaison with mentors and judges.

Creative Designer

  • Visual and physical design.
  • Sketches and 3D models.
  • Makes the solution easy and pleasant to use.

Technical Expert

  • Electronics, code and data.
  • Leads the use of AI coding tools and checks what they produce.
  • Makes data-driven decisions.

Communicator

  • Pitching and storytelling.
  • Documents the journey (photo, video, AI use log).
  • Shows the real impact of the project.

Roles organize the work, they do not limit what you learn: every team member must be able to explain how the whole project works.

Design Challenge Rubric

Teams are evaluated in three categories: Ideate (30%), Craft (40%) and Share (30%). Maximum total: 40 points. Each criterion is scored from 1 to 4.

1. IDEATE (30% - 12 pts max)

Evaluates how well you understood the problem and how promising and original your idea is.

Criteria Beginning (1 pt) Developed (2 pts) Accomplished (3 pts) Exemplary (4 pts)
Problem Understanding Describes the problem in general terms; no local data or affected people identified Identifies who is affected and uses some general information about the problem Uses real local data or observations and explains who is affected and why it matters Shows deep understanding built on their own measurements or interviews, and a precise, testable problem statement
Relevance & Impact Potential Loosely connected to the chosen problem; unclear who would use it Addresses the chosen problem; the benefit is described but not realistic yet Clearly solves the chosen problem, links to the relevant SDGs, and could be used in a real school Realistic, scalable solution with transformative potential that a school could adopt tomorrow
Creativity & Originality Basic idea; follows the most obvious solution Some creative thinking; attempts a new approach Original concept and unique way of solving the problem Highly innovative solution with exceptional originality and fresh perspectives

2. CRAFT (40% - 16 pts max)

Evaluates whether it works, whether you can prove it, and how well you used AI tools to build it.

Criteria Beginning (1 pt) Developed (2 pts) Accomplished (3 pts) Exemplary (4 pts)
Working Solution A prototype with some working parts A prototype that identifies which parts work and which do not A prototype that works with few bugs and has a plan for future development A prototype that works reliably, in real conditions, and articulates clear next iterations
Evidence of Impact No measurements or user tests; claims are based on opinion Some data or a single informal test Clear data or user tests that show the solution does what it claims, with a comparison (for example before and after, or against an official source) Convincing evidence, with limits and uncertainty honestly stated, showing real benefit for real users
Strategic Use of AI Tools Uses AI to generate code or content but accepts the results without checking Uses one AI tool for one purpose and checks some of its output Uses AI tools for different purposes (for example coding, data analysis, design) and verifies and corrects the results Chooses the right AI tool for each task, writes strong prompts, catches AI mistakes, and can say where AI helped and where it did not
Iteration & Troubleshooting Minimal testing or troubleshooting evident Some evidence of testing and problem-solving Clear documentation of testing cycles and the improvements they led to Comprehensive troubleshooting journey with several iterations, each based on evidence

3. SHARE (30% - 12 pts max)

Evaluates whether every team member understands the project, how well the process is documented, and how clearly you communicate.

Criteria Beginning (1 pt) Developed (2 pts) Accomplished (3 pts) Exemplary (4 pts)
Understanding & Ownership Only one or two members can explain how the solution works Most members explain what the solution does but not how it works or why decisions were made All members explain how it works and justify key decisions when judges ask questions All members explain the whole project in depth, including what they would change and why, and answer unexpected questions confidently
Process & AI Use Log Minimal evidence of the design thinking stages; no record of AI use Some documentation of key steps; a partial list of AI tools used Documentation of all five stages, plus a log of which AI tools were used, for what, and what had to be corrected A compelling record of the whole journey, with an honest AI log that includes prompts that failed and what the team learned from them
Presentation & Communication Information is unclear or hard to follow Communicates the main ideas, with room for improvement Clear, organized and engaging presentation of the problem, solution and results Masterful storytelling that captivates the audience and makes the impact unmistakable

Driving Principles & Rules

Success for All

Encourage high-quality work, respect individuals and the community. Collaboration between competitors.

Overcome Obstacles

Learn from mistakes, accept frustration, and persevere.

You Own What You Submit

AI is your tool, not your teammate. You are responsible for everything you present and you must be able to explain it.

Rules & Regulations Summary

  • Behave properly: respect, responsibility, caring, trustworthiness.
  • Technology is for communication, never to offend.
  • Use official channels for communication.
  • Practice safe digital citizenship: do not paste personal data of other people into AI tools.
  • Keep a log of the AI tools you use.
  • Sessions may be recorded.
